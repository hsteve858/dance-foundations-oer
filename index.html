Description

Dance Foundations: An OER Course Collection brings together open dance curriculums for Ballet I, Tap I, Hip Hop I, Dance Composition & Improv, and Dance for Musical Theatre. Developed by theatre and dance college faculty, the collection includes open lesson plans, and curated learning resources, for students, instructors, and dance educators. Each chapter in this book corresponds to one course.

About the Sway Course Materials

Many weekly lessons are presented using Microsoft Sways. Each Sway organizes the week's instructional materials, practice guidance, readings, questions for reflection, activities and more.

Chapter 1: Ballet I (DAN 1031)

Ballet Shoes / CC BY

Welcome

This open resource was created to align with the learning outcomes of Ballet I and serves as an instructional guide to support learning throughout the course.

Course Description

Introduces the basic techniques of ballet, which are built upon knowledge of ballet terminology, fundamental exercises, and the basic elements of dance. Focuses on movement-oriented dance, consisting of stretching, barre warm-up exercises, simple Terre à Terre and jumping steps, and basic extended positions.

Course Schedule

Week 1 Introduction to Ballet: Tradition, Discipline, Creativity

Introduces ballet foundations through tradition, discipline, and creativity, covering history, safety, alignment, basic positions, terminology, and introductory exercises. Includes reflection questions, videos, and at-home practice to build foundational skills, body awareness, and safe movement habits.

Week 2 Creativity within Classical Structure

Introduces battement tendu at the barre, building on first and second position, demi-plié, and port de bras from Week 1. Students read about the political and social origins of court ballet (Catherine de Medici, Louis XIV, and the transition from court social dances to theatrical ballet) and learn new vocabulary including tendu, battement, devant, and à la seconde. A full barre pattern combines pliés and tendus with arm coordination, and a creativity exercise invites students to make and justify individual choices in dynamics, focus, and breath within the set tendu sequence, reinforcing the week's theme that creativity can flourish within classical structure.

Week 3 Grace, Extension & Balance

Builds on Weeks 1–2 (alignment, demi-plié, tendu, and basic coordination) and introduces two new steps, dégagé and an introduction to rond de jambe à terre, along with simple balances at the barre, with an overall focus on lengthening lines and smoothing transitions while maintaining safe, organized technique.

Week 4 Building the Barre Routine

This lesson puts previously learned steps, demi-plié, tendu, dégagé, and rond de jambe, together into one consistent barre sequence. The goal is for the barre to begin feeling like a familiar ritual, connecting personal discipline to broader ballet tradition. Students will read and reflect on an article on the role and purpose of barre within ballet training.

Week 5 Adagio & Line

Building on pliés, tendus, dégagés, rond de jambe, and basic balances from prior weeks, this lesson introduces simple adagio (slow, sustained movement) at both the barre and center, with an emphasis on longer lines, extensions, and using breath for control and grace.

Week 6 Petite Allegro & Rhythm

Introduces small jumps (petite allegro) — sauté, changement, échappé — with an emphasis on safe landings, plié for shock absorption, and rhythmic awareness, supported by a reading on improving petit allegro technique. Students practice jump combinations at varying tempos and explore contrasting dynamic qualities (light/buoyant vs. grounded/powerful) within the same steps.

Week 7 Coordination & Musical Phrasing

Reviews and refines coordination of arms, legs, and head while linking barre vocabulary into center combinations that combine adagio and petite allegro qualities. Includes practice counting music aloud, attention to spatial awareness and group spacing, and a small-group task creating short phrases with a directional change and a moment of balance, in preparation for the Week 8 midterm.

Week 8 Midterm Check-in

A formative assessment week featuring a barre sequence (pliés, tendus, dégagés, rond de jambe, balance) and a center combination blending adagio and petite allegro, evaluated on alignment, musicality, and coordination. Includes a written reflection and a take-home essay assignment connecting readings on ballet history (royal courts, professionalization) to the technique and classroom structure experienced in the course.

Week 9 Traveling & Phrase Design

Introduces traveling steps, ballet walks, chassés, and the balancé/waltz step, along with directional vocabulary (devant, derrière, en avant, en arrière). Includes a video-based observation of a classical variation and a design challenge in which small groups create a traveling phrase incorporating a change of direction, a change of level, and a dynamic contrast.

Week 10 Music, Rhythm & Movement

Explores how the same steps feel and function differently across musical tempos and meters (2/4, 3/4, 4/4), supported by a reading surveying international ballet traditions and choreographers (British, Russian, French). Barre, center, and traveling exercises are repeated in contrasting rhythms, and students experiment with adding personal musical accents to familiar phrases.

Week 11 Strength, Endurance & Healthy Technique

Focuses on building stamina through slightly longer barre and center combinations, with attention to breath, pacing, and body awareness to support injury prevention. Includes short instructional videos on ballet breathing and performance stamina, integrated conditioning exercises (relevés, calf raises), and reflection prompts connecting strength training to sustainable, healthy technique.

Week 12 Somatic Integration & Injury Prevention

Introduces a somatic (body-mind awareness) approach to technique, emphasizing internal sensation, breath, weight distribution, and proprioception over external appearance, supported by a video from a dance medicine specialist. A slower-paced barre and center class incorporates guided body scans and self-selected somatic focus points to build injury awareness and self-regulation.

Week 13 Performance Preparation

Sets a synthesizing 32-count center phrase combining tendu, adagio, petite allegro, and traveling steps learned throughout the course. Students rehearse in small groups, run the phrase individually, and practice giving and receiving structured, specific peer feedback, alongside somatic and mental preparation cues for performing under observation.

Week 14 Informal Showing & Feedback

Students perform the Week 13 performance phrase in small groups for the class, using a structured peer-response protocol addressing technique/alignment, musicality/rhythm, and artistry/line. Includes guided self-highlight reflections after each showing and a class debrief connecting the experience back to the course's tradition, discipline, and creativity themes.

Week 15 Final Practical & Reflection

Concludes the course with a final practical demonstration combining a full barre sequence and the 32-count center phrase, assessed on participation, progress, musicality, and safety. Students give brief self-highlights after performing, join a full-class ensemble run of the phrase, and complete a summative written or recorded reflection connecting their growth to personal and course learning outcomes.

Chapter 2: Tap I (DAN 1043)

Tap dancing class in the gymnasium / Public Domain.

Welcome

This open resource was created to align with the learning outcomes of Tap I and serves as an instructional guide to support learning throughout the course.

Course Description

Introduces basic tap dance movements and techniques. The shuffle, ball change, brush, flap heel drop, stomp, and stamp step are covered.[1]

Week 1: Tap Foundations

Week 1 Overview

Week 1 introduces students to the foundational elements of tap dance, including proper footwear, terminology, posture, classroom etiquette, weight shifting, balance, and sound awareness. Students begin developing the connection between movement and rhythm through basic clapping exercises, foot articulation, and introductory tap vocabulary while gaining an understanding of tap as both a dance form and a form of musical expression.

Week 1 Topics

  • Course introduction and expectations.
  • Tap shoes, terminology, posture, and classroom etiquette.
  • Introduce the idea of tap as both dance and rhythm.
  • Basic weight shifting, balance, and sound awareness.
  • Begin simple rhythm clapping and foot articulation.

    Chapter 3: Hip Hop I (DAN 1005)

    Paris Salsa Hip Hop Battle 2019 / CC BY

    Welcome

    This open resource was created to align with the learning outcomes of Hip Hop I and serves as an instructional guide to support learning throughout the course.

    Course Description

    Consist of basic concepts and skill of hip hop dance. Focuses on warm-up exercises, street dance footwork, ground moves, isolations and freezes. Explores hip hop dance as a high energy expression of hip hop culture. Students will learn combinations leading to hip-hop dance routines.

    Course Topic Outline

    I. Defining Hip Hop at a Beginning Level

    Technique, Vocabulary, Culture, History, Influences, Popularization

    II. Fundamental Stylistic Exercises

    Warm-Up, Center Floor, Across the Floor exercises; Body Awareness and Alignment;

    Strength, Coordination, & Flexibility; Rhythm and Weight Change; Quality

    III. Creation and Performance

    Course Schedule

    Week 1: Introduction to Hip Hop Dance & Culture

    Reviews course information, learning objectives, and assignments, introducing students to hip hop culture and dance through key elements (DJing, MCing, Breaking, Graffiti, Knowledge), the origins and social context of hip hop, foundational dance styles taught in the course, basic grooves and introductory footwork, and course expectations and structure.

    Weeks 2 & 3: Party Grooves

    Guides students through party grooves as social dances by distinguishing up, down, bounce, and drop groove categories, connecting named steps to their cultural and social origins, observing how hip hop dance is represented in media, and beginning a response paper on the origins of social grooves in hip hop dance.

    Week 4: Freestyle and Cypher

    Guides students through freestyle and cypher practice by explaining what a cypher is and why it matters in hip hop culture, connecting response paper ideas to class study, applying groove categories in freestyle movement, and building confidence entering and exiting small and large cyphers with awareness and respect.

    Weeks 5, 6, 7: Breakin

    This 3-week unit explores the origins of breaking in 1970s Bronx hip hop culture and introduces the structure of a breaking round: top rock, go downs, floorwork, and freeze. Students build skills week-by-week from foundational top rock patterns to floor entries, basic floorwork, and the baby freeze, finishing up by combining these elements into a short breaking flow and explaining how breaking's history shaped what they learned.

    Week 8 (Midterm): Cypher

    During Week 8 students will demonstrate their skills through a freestyle cypher, entering and exiting the circle while applying grooves and breaking vocabulary developed throughout the term. Discussions on the cypher's cultural role in hip hop dance connect studio practice to historical sources, including Pabon's "Physical Graffiti" and Durden's documentary, in a midterm paper.

    Weeks 9 & 10: Electric Boogaloo

    Leads students through Electric Boogaloo history and technique by describing its 1970s West Coast funk roots, identifying founding members of the Electric Boogaloos and their contributions, exploring illusion-based styles such as popping, waving, robot, animation, and isolation, and building foundational popping technique through hits, waves, and control-based drills.

    Weeks 11 & 12: Locking

    Explores the origins of locking and Don "Campbellock" Campbell's role in creating it. Introduced are key members of The Lockers and their influence on the style, and students will learn how locking connects to funk music, Soul Train, and audience performance. Includes a foundational locking vocabulary, up-lock, point, wrist twirl, and stop n go, combined into short phrases while comparing locking to other forms studied in class.

    Weeks 13 & 14: Commercial Hip Hop Choreography

    Takes students through commercial hip hop choreography by discussing how the style reflects popular culture and the entertainment industry. Students learn and retain two hip hop combinations, identify grooves, textures, levels, and performance skills, and reflect on how commercial dance differs from freestyle, cypher, and social dance forms.

    Week 15: FINAL

    Brings students through a semester-ending reflection by warming up with intention, participating in a class cypher that draws on movement from the whole term, and learning a final commercial hip hop combination. Students then reflect on the major units studied and identify key ideas, vocabulary, and cultural insights for their final reflection paper.

    Chapter 4: Dance Composition & Improv (DAN 2011)

    The Latin dance group, Ballet Folkorico Mexicano de / Public Domain

    Welcome

    This open resource was created to align with the learning outcomes of Dance Composition & Improv and serves as an instructional guide to support learning throughout the course.

    Course Description

    Includes creative problem-solving and choreographic exercises to explore the basic elements of dance. The course introduces improvisation and focuses on developing skills in inventing and structuring movement to create original works of choreography.

    Course Schedule

    Weeks 1–2: Introduction to Composition and Improvisation

    Introduces choreography as a process rooted in observation, decision-making, and improvisation rather than fixed steps. Students study the movement approaches of Cunningham, Dunham, and Forsythe, engage in improvisation scores built around time, space, and energy, and develop a short 20–30 second phrase demonstrating spatial intention. Includes readings on the history and practice of improvisation, guided studio labs, peer feedback, and a written reflection assignment connecting movement discoveries to the assigned readings and viewings.

    Weeks 3–4: Generating Movement Material

    Focuses on generating original solo movement through structured improvisation scores (single body part, gesture, pathway, and emotional/visual prompts) and then shaping that material using repetition, variation, and contrast. Students build a short improvisational solo study with a clear movement theme, journal about emerging choreographic ideas, and participate in in-class showings with structured peer feedback.

    Weeks 5–6: Building Form from Improvisation

    Guides students in translating improvisational material into a structured solo using compositional forms such as ABA and theme-and-variation. Students write a choreographic intent statement, make intentional choices about transitions, entrances, exits, and the relationship between movement and music or silence, and refine a 60–90 second draft solo through small-group showings and peer feedback.

    Week 7: Solo Refinement & Rehearsal Check-Ins

    No Sway this week.

    • Watch: The Process of a Choreographer: A New Way to View Dance | Steven Butler | TEDxPasadena
    • Read: Liz Lerman’s Critical Response Process

    Week 8: Midterm Solo & Performance and Critical Response Process

    Centers on a midterm solo showing paired with an in-depth introduction to Liz Lerman's Critical Response Process (CRP). Building on the CRP reading from Week 7, students perform their prepared solos, then practice giving and receiving feedback through CRP's four steps: statements of meaning, artist as questioner, neutral questions, and permissioned opinions. Includes readings and a video on CRP, discussion questions distinguishing neutral questions from opinions, and a written reflection on choreographic intent, feedback received, and next steps for revision.

    Weeks 9–10: Group Improvisation & Unison

    Shifts the course focus from solo to group work, introducing group improvisation strategies and the distinction between unison and canon. Students explore spatial relationships among dancers, proximity, direction, levels, and pathways, through small-group improvisation exercises and create short group studies incorporating time, space, and energy.

    Weeks 11–13: From Solos to Ensemble

    A three-week arc that takes students from task-based group improvisation through thematic development to an oral progress critique of a full group piece. Students translate solo material into ensemble phrases, apply structures like ABA or theme-and-variation to group work, consider how costume and lighting can support choreographic meaning, and give/receive feedback in a structured oral critique. Assignments include a 3–4 minute group piece draft, a written statement of intent, and short analytical responses to professional dance viewings (e.g., Alvin Ailey's Revelations, Twyla Tharp's In the Upper Room).

    Week 14: Technical Rehearsal and Revision

    A rehearsal-focused week where student groups apply technical notes, covering rehearsal efficiency, lighting cues, costuming, and spatial staging, to refine their final group compositions ahead of the culminating performance.

    Week 15: Final Group Performance and Evaluation

    The course's culminating week, in which each group performs a polished, performance-ready composition (3–5 minutes) demonstrating clear intent, coherent structure, and ensemble timing. Students apply Liz Lerman's Critical Response Process and oral progress critique skills to give live feedback after each performance, then complete written peer evaluations along with a self-reflection on their growth since the Week 8 midterm.

    Chapter 5: Dance for Musical Theatre (DAN 2024)

    Usher_Hall_interior by Brian McNeil / CC BY 3.0

    Welcome

    This open resource was created to align with the learning outcomes of Dance for Musical Theatre and serves as an instructional guide to support learning throughout the course.

    Course Description

    Introduces students to dance within the context of musical theatre. Enables the student to practice non-verbal communication and expressive movement techniques.

    Course Topic Outline

    Historical development of dance in musical theatre; choreographic elements in musical theatre; musical theatre production (components, planning, the dance chorus); application of choreographic concepts (composition, purpose, development, practice); performance preparation (rehearsal techniques, feedback, elements of theatre production).

    Course Schedule

    Week 1 Dance for Musical Theatre

    Introduces the semester with syllabus review, class norms, and a foundational warm-up (releases, cardio, center barre, floor stretches). Students read and discuss Zachary Dorsey's "Dance and Choreography," learning a three-part method for analyzing musical-theatre dance, description, synthesis, and interpretation, applied to the number "Choreography" from White Christmas. Introduces across-the-floor vocabulary including pas de bourré, chassés, 3-step turns, kick-ball-change, and drags.

    Weeks 2 & 3 Golden Age of Musical Theatre

    Explores key features of the Golden Age "book musical" and the ways dance became an integrated part of story, character, and music. Major trends in Golden Age choreography are examined, with particular attention to Agnes de Mille and Jerome Robbins. Center work and across-the-floor combinations provide opportunities to develop technique, followed by a combination inspired by the Golden Age musical theatre style.

    Week 4 & 5 Tap

    Introduces the history, technique, and cultural significance of tap dance within musical theatre. The unit traces tap's African and European roots through vaudeville, film, Broadway, and contemporary practice, highlighting key artists, major historical shifts, and the contributions of women to the form. Technical instruction covers foundational vocabulary, rhythm, sound clarity, and Broadway-style combinations.

    Weeks 6 & 7 Jazz

    Explores the cultural roots and technique of jazz dance through collaborative reading and studio training, tracing its history (Harlem Renaissance, Charleston, Lindy Hop) and characteristics (syncopation, isolations, improvisation). Traces Broadway's shift from Golden Age storytelling to the concept musical and Mega Musical eras, using A Chorus Line and Cats as case studies of choreographers Michael Bennett and Dame Gillian Lynne.

    Week 8: Midterm

    Weeks 9 & 10 Fosse

    Explores Bob Fosse's choreographic legacy through discussion and jazz technique training. Students examine how Fosse's physicality and self-image shaped his signature style, turned-in legs, isolations, hand articulation, and controlled stillness, and discuss his impact on Broadway ensemble choreography across shows like Sweet Charity, Pippin, and Chicago.

    Weeks 11 & 12 Hip Hop on Broadway

    Explores hip hop's cultural origins and its entry into Broadway musical theatre. Students study hip hop's roots in 1970s Bronx culture and its core elements (DJing, MCing, breaking, graffiti, knowledge), then examine through readings and video how shows like In the Heights and Hamilton adapted hip hop for the stage, and discuss tensions between authenticity and theatrical presentation.

    Weeks 13 Contemporary

    Explores the range of contemporary Broadway choreography through viewing works by concert dance choreographers including Justin Peck, Camille A. Brown, and Bill T. Jones. Students examine how movement blending ballet, modern, and release technique conveys an emotional atmosphere rather than pure technique, comparing styles across shows like Illinoise, Hell's Kitchen, and Jagged Little Pill.

    Week 14 & 15 Audition Unit: Dance Calls

    Prepares students for professional musical theatre auditions through mock dance calls and callbacks. Drawing on Neil Rutherford's Musical Theatre Auditions and Casting, students learn dance call structure, audition etiquette, room awareness, and what to bring and wear. The unit progresses through a mock dance audition, an audition checklist assignment, a mock callback with new choreography under pressure, and 16-bar song cuts.
